Lithium-ion batteries degrade faster at elevated temperatures. When cells were cycled at 45 degrees Celsius, capacity fell by 18 percent after 500 cycles, compared with only 6 percent at 25 degrees Celsius.

The accelerated loss is driven by growth of the solid-electrolyte interphase. Because higher temperatures speed up the side reactions that consume lithium, the interphase thickens and the usable capacity declines.

We measured an internal resistance increase of 2.224 milliohms over the same cycle window. These results are consistent with prior reports of thermally driven aging.

The model predicts that operating the pack below 30 degrees Celsius would extend service life by more than 40 percent. This prediction has not yet been validated experimentally.
