This study aims to explore the potential targets and anticancer mechanisms of dendrobine from Dendrobium nobile in the treatment of colorectal cancer through network pharmacology, and to experimentally validate its specific effects.

Initially, potential targets of dendrobine were identified using the ITCM Traditional Chinese Medicine database, while colorectal cancer-related genes were obtained from the NCBI Gene database, with the intersection of these datasets taken for further analysis. Functional enrichment analysis was conducted using the Metascape database, and a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed. Additionally, cell culture, cell proliferation assays, and wound healing assays were performed. The Wnt/β-catenin and NF-κB/COX-2/PGE2 signaling pathways were analyzed using PCR and Western blot experiments.

The PPI network constructed from 152 intersecting genes revealed that these genes play crucial roles in processes such as cell proliferation, apoptosis, and signal transduction. Cell-based assays demonstrated that dendrobine significantly inhibits the proliferation and migration of colorectal cancer cells. Furthermore, PCR and Western blot results indicated that dendrobine suppresses colorectal cancer cell proliferation and migration by modulating the Wnt/β-catenin and NF-κB/COX-2/PGE2 signaling pathways.

Dendrobine exhibits significant anticancer potential against colorectal cancer by regulating the Wnt/β-catenin and NF-κB/COX-2/PGE2 signaling pathways, providing a theoretical foundation and experimental evidence for its therapeutic application in colorectal cancer.

Colorectal cancer (CRC) ranks among the most prevalent and lethal malignancies globally, with recent cancer statistics indicating approximately 1.9 million new cases and 900,000 deaths annually, a number that continues to rise as populations age and risk factors such as diet and lifestyle become more widespread [–]. While considerable advancements have been made in early detection and treatment modalities, including improved screening programs and minimally invasive surgical techniques, the overall prognosis for CRC patients remains poor [, ]. This is largely due to the intricate and multifactorial nature of CRC's pathogenesis, as well as the high rates of recurrence and metastasis that accompany advanced stages of the disease [].

Current therapeutic approaches, including surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and targeted therapies, have undoubtedly improved survival outcomes for some patients. However, these treatments are often accompanied by significant adverse effects, including toxicity and organ damage, and are frequently complicated by the emergence of drug resistance, particularly in the case of advanced or metastatic CRC [, ]. Consequently, many patients experience limited long-term benefits, underlining the urgent need for the development of new, safer, and more effective treatment strategies that can overcome these limitations and provide durable responses.

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), with its complex composition and multi-target effects, has garnered increasing attention in cancer treatment in recent years. Dendrobine, an alkaloid compound extracted from the orchid Dendrobium nobile, exhibits various biological activities, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and immunomodulatory effects []. Preliminary studies have shown that dendrobine displays significant anticancer activity in lung cancer [, ]; however, its specific molecular mechanisms in colorectal cancer have yet to be fully elucidated.

Network pharmacology is an interdisciplinary approach that integrates systems biology, computational biology, and pharmacology to construct drug-target-disease networks, thereby uncovering the mechanisms of action and multi-target characteristics of drugs []. This method not only allows for the systematic analysis of drug-target interactions but also utilizes bioinformatics tools to predict potential drug targets and associated signaling pathways, offering new perspectives and methodologies for TCM research [].

This study aims to systematically explore the potential targets and molecular mechanisms of dendrobine in the treatment of colorectal cancer using network pharmacology. By screening and analyzing the potential targets of dendrobine in conjunction with colorectal cancer-related genes, we constructed a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network and conducted functional enrichment analysis to reveal its biological role in colorectal cancer. Furthermore, we validated the anticancer mechanisms of dendrobine through cell-based and signaling pathway experiments, with a focus on the Wnt/β-catenin [] and NF-κB/COX-2/PGE2 signaling pathways []. This study not only aids in uncovering the multi-target characteristics of dendrobine in CRC treatment but also provides a theoretical foundation and experimental evidence for its clinical application. We hope that this research will offer new insights and strategies for the use of dendrobine in the treatment of colorectal cancer.

Potential targets of dendrobine were screened from the ITCM Traditional Chinese Medicine database. The keyword "dendrobine" was entered to automatically retrieve relevant biological targets from the database. Detailed information about these targets, including gene names, protein names, and supporting literature, was then downloaded.

Colorectal cancer-related genes were obtained from the NCBI Gene database. Using "colorectal cancer" as a keyword, a search was conducted, and a list of genes significantly associated with colorectal cancer was extracted. Detailed information about these genes, including gene names, functional descriptions, and related literature, was also downloaded.

An intersection analysis was conducted on the two datasets using the R programming language. Dendrobine targets from the ITCM database and colorectal cancer-related genes from the NCBI Gene database were imported into the R environment. The intersection function was used to identify common genes, resulting in a total of 152 intersecting genes.

The identified intersecting genes were input into the Metaspase database to generate a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network. The Metaspase database provides reliable protein interaction information. By inputting the gene list, the corresponding PPI network was generated. In the generated PPI network, parameters such as degree centrality and closeness centrality of nodes were analyzed to identify key nodes and important interactions.

Functional enrichment analysis of the intersecting genes was performed using the Metaspase database. The intersecting gene list was input, and gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was conducted for three categories: biological process (BP), molecular function (MF), and cellular component (CC). GO analysis was performed using the Gene Ontology database (http://geneontology.org/). Additionally, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis was performed to explore the potential roles of these genes in different biological processes and signaling pathways, using the KEGG database (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/).

The HT29 cell line used in this study was purchased from Shanghai Jinyuan Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Catalog number: JY198), and the SW480 cell line (Catalog number: JY119) was also obtained from the same source. Both the HT29 and SW480 human colorectal cancer cell lines were used for culture. Cells were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) in a 37 °C, 5% CO2 incubator. The medium was changed every 2–3 days, and cells were passaged as needed.

To determine the IC50 values of dendrobine, HT29 and SW480 cells were cultured for 24 h at a density of 5000 cells per well in a 96-well plate. The cells were then treated with various concentrations of dendrobine (ranging from 0.1 to 100 μM) for 24 h. Cell viability was measured using the CCK-8 assay, following the manufacturer's instructions. Specifically, 10 μL of the CCK-8 solution was added to each well, and the plate was incubated for 2 h at 37 °C. The absorbance at 450 nm was then measured using a Bio-Rad microplate reader. The IC50 values were calculated by plotting the percentage of cell viability against the dendrobine concentration using GraphPad Prism software.

A CCK-8 assay was used to assess the effect of dendrobine on cell proliferation. Colorectal cancer cells were seeded into 96-well plates. After cells adhered, they were treated with varying concentrations of dendrobine for 24, 48, and 72 h. At each time point, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution was added, and after a 2-h incubation, the absorbance was measured at 450 nm to calculate the inhibition rate of cell proliferation.

The effect of dendrobine on cell migration was observed using a wound healing assay. HT29 and SW480 cells were seeded into 6-well plates, and once the cells reached over 90% confluence, a scratch was made on the cell monolayer using a sterile pipette tip. After washing away the floating cells with PBS, culture media containing different concentrations of dendrobine were added. Images of the scratch width were taken at 0, 24, and 48 h, and the cell migration rate was calculated for both HT29 and SW480 cell lines.

After blocking nonspecific binding sites, primary and secondary antibodies were incubated sequentially. Protein bands were detected by chemiluminescence, and relative expression levels of target proteins were calculated using GAPDH as an internal control. The IC50 value of dendrobine against colorectal cancer cells was determined using the CCK8 assay after 24 h of treatment (Fig. ). The x-axis represents the logarithmic concentration of dendrobine (Log C [Dendrobium nobile dendrobium] μM), while the y-axis represents the percentage of cell viability. As the concentration of dendrobine increased, cell viability gradually decreased, demonstrating a typical dose–response relationship. Based on the fitted curve, the IC50 value of dendrobine was calculated to be 5.769 μM, indicating that at this concentration, dendrobine can inhibit the growth of 50% of colorectal cancer cells. This result provides fundamental data for further investigation into the anticancer mechanisms of dendrobine.

Cell proliferation was assessed using the CCK8 assay (Fig. ). The x-axis represents time (hours), and the y-axis represents relative proliferation rate. HT29 cells (Fig. A) and SW480 cells (Fig. B) were used for the assay. The blue curve indicates cell proliferation under normal culture conditions, the purple curve represents the MSAB group (working concentration: 10 μM), and the red curve represents the dendrobine group (working concentration: 5.769 μM). At the time points of 0, 24, 48, and 72 h, the cell proliferation rate in the normal culture group was significantly higher than that in the MSAB and dendrobine groups. The cell proliferation rate in the dendrobine-treated group was similar to that in the MSAB-treated group, and both were significantly lower than that in the normal culture group (p < 0.0001). These results suggest that dendrobine effectively inhibits the proliferation of colorectal cancer cells, demonstrating its potential anticancer activity.

This study systematically investigated the potential targets of dendrobine in the treatment of colorectal cancer using network pharmacology, and validated its anti-cancer mechanisms through cellular and signaling pathway experiments. The results demonstrate that dendrobine exhibits significant anti-colorectal cancer activity, providing new perspectives and theoretical foundations for its application in cancer therapy.

Firstly, through the intersection analysis of the ITCM database and NCBI Gene database, we identified 152 potential targets of dendrobine related to colorectal cancer. The PPI network constructed using the Metaspase database showed complex interactions among these genes, with key nodes such as CTNNB1 (β-catenin), COX-2, and TP53 exhibiting high centrality in the network. This suggests that these genes play important roles in the occurrence and development of colorectal cancer, which is consistent with previous studies and further supports their critical role in colorectal cancer []. The functional enrichment analysis from the Metaspase database revealed that these intersecting genes are mainly involved in biological processes such as cell proliferation, apoptosis, and signaling pathways. GO and KEGG analyses indicated that these genes participate in several key signaling pathways, including Wnt/β-catenin, NF-κB, and PI3K-Akt. The Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway plays a crucial role in maintaining cell proliferation and differentiation, and its aberrant activation is closely related to the development of various cancers [, ]. The NF-κB signaling pathway is a key regulator of inflammation and cell survival, with COX-2 and PGE2 playing significant roles in the formation of the inflammatory cancer microenvironment. These pathways are essential in colorectal cancer [], providing direction for further research into the anti-cancer mechanisms of dendrobine.

The IC50 value of dendrobine for colorectal cancer cells was determined to be 5.769 μM through CCK8 assays, indicating that dendrobine inhibits 50% of colorectal cancer cell growth at this concentration. This value is consistent with other studies on natural compounds with anti-cancer properties, where IC50 values often range within the low micromolar concentrations, suggesting significant cytotoxicity against cancer cells. Further CCK8 results demonstrated that dendrobine significantly inhibits colorectal cancer cell proliferation, consistent with its high connectivity score in the HT29 cell line. These findings align with earlier studies that explored the anti-proliferative effects of alkaloids in various cancer cell types, where natural compounds disrupted cell cycle progression and induced apoptosis.

Scratch wound assays further revealed that dendrobine significantly inhibits colorectal cancer cell migration, suggesting its potential to reduce cancer metastasis and invasion by inhibiting cell migration. Inhibition of migration is critical in preventing metastasis, a hallmark of aggressive cancers. Similar anti-migratory effects have been observed in other studies investigating the role of plant-derived compounds, such as curcumin [, ] and berberine [], in cancer treatment. These experimental results align with network pharmacology analysis, indicating that dendrobine exerts its anti-cancer effects by regulating the expression of key genes and proteins.

PCR and Western blot experiments confirmed the regulatory effects of dendrobine on the Wnt/β-catenin and NF-κB/COX-2/PGE2 signaling pathways. PCR results showed that dendrobine treatment significantly reduced the mRNA expression of key Wnt/β-catenin pathway genes β-catenin, c-myc, and cyclin D1, which play central roles in cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival in colorectal cancer. In addition, dendrobine downregulated the mRNA expression of NF-κB, COX-2, and PGE2 in the NF-κB pathway, which is known to be involved in inflammatory responses, cell survival, and tumor progression. Western blot results further validated these changes at the protein level, indicating that dendrobine achieves its anti-cancer effects by inhibiting these key signaling pathways.

This study systematically reveals the potential targets and molecular mechanisms of dendrobine in colorectal cancer treatment by combining network pharmacology with experimental validation. The results show that dendrobine not only significantly inhibits colorectal cancer cell proliferation and migration but also exerts its anti-cancer effects by regulating the Wnt/β-catenin and NF-κB/COX-2/PGE2 signaling pathways. The involvement of multiple signaling pathways suggests that dendrobine has a multi-targeted approach to cancer inhibition, which could potentially reduce the likelihood of cancer cells developing resistance, a major issue in chemotherapy. This provides new ideas and theoretical basis for the development of dendrobine as a colorectal cancer therapeutic agent.

Future research could further explore the anti-cancer effects of dendrobine in in vivo models and assess its safety and efficacy through clinical trials. Additionally, more in-depth studies on the molecular mechanisms could reveal the potential role of dendrobine in regulating the tumor microenvironment, such as immune cell infiltration and stromal interactions, as well as its effects on drug resistance mechanisms. Such research may advance the clinical application of dendrobine in colorectal cancer treatment, offering new therapeutic options for patients and contributing to the development of novel, more effective treatments for combating cancer.

This study combined network pharmacology and experimental validation to elucidate the potential targets and underlying anti-cancer mechanisms of dendrobine in colorectal cancer treatment. The results demonstrate that dendrobine exerts its anti-cancer effects primarily through modulation of the Wnt/β-catenin and NF-κB/COX-2/PGE2 signaling pathways, resulting in significant inhibition of colorectal cancer cell proliferation and migration. These findings underscore dendrobine’s substantial therapeutic potential and lay a strong foundation for future research aimed at developing dendrobine as an effective treatment option for colorectal cancer.
